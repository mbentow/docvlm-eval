Add selective prediction: coverage vs accuracy, with a guard against fake curves

Accuracy alone does not say whether a pipeline is deployable. The question is
"if I route the least-confident 20% to a human, what accuracy remains on the
rest", and the answer can differ sharply from the headline number: on the
bundled hard corpus the 8b run scores 0.583 overall, yet its most-confident
half is 1.000 with no errors escaping review.

- risk-coverage curve, AURC, accuracy_at(coverage) and coverage_for(accuracy)
- AURC compared against the mean of 25 random orderings; when the confidence
  signal ranks no better than shuffling, the report says so rather than drawing
  a persuasive curve a review policy would then be built on
- hallucination rate carried along the curve, since coverage that holds accuracy
  while concentrating invented values is not a win
- confidence from a declared schema field, else the mean field score

Also adds named image preprocessing profiles, hashed into the config so they
reach the cache key and the run provenance. They ship with a negative result:
upscaling regressed accuracy and doubled latency on this corpus.

Ties in the confidence ranking were initially broken by input order, because
sorted() is stable — a constant confidence signal inherited the corpus order and
produced a curve that looked informative while ranking nothing. Caught by the
test that asserts a useless signal is reported as useless.
